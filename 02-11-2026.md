# PRIME LABORATORY SYSTEM (PLS)

## Enterprise Audit, Pharmacy Accounting Separation, Modular Architecture and Google Analytics–Inspired Dashboard Redesign

### YOUR ROLE

Act as a senior enterprise software architect, financial systems engineer, full-stack developer, UI/UX designer, and healthcare information systems auditor.

You are working on **Prime Laboratory System (PLS)**, a modular, multi-tenant healthcare management platform.

Your assignment is to perform a comprehensive audit and improvement of the following:

1. Pharmacy module and its independent accounting system.
2. Administrative desktop analytics and dashboards.
3. Pharmacy analytics and financial reporting.
4. Strict separation between pharmacy, laboratory, imaging, inventory and other modules.
5. Module-based subscription, access control and authorization.
6. Enterprise-grade data visualization inspired by Google Analytics 4.

**Important:** Do not immediately start rewriting the application. First inspect the existing codebase, understand the architecture, identify the underlying problems, document your findings and develop a safe implementation plan. Then implement approved or clearly scoped improvements without breaking existing functionality.

# PART 1: STRICT PHARMACY ACCOUNTING SEPARATION

The pharmacy must have its own fully functional accounting section, independent of the laboratory accounting interface.

The current application appears to mix pharmacy analytics, financial accounting information and laboratory-related data. This must be investigated and corrected.

### 1.1 Pharmacy Accounting Requirements

The pharmacy must have a dedicated accounting section containing:

- Pharmacy revenue and sales ledger.
- Cost of goods sold (COGS).
- Gross profit and gross profit margin.
- Operating expenses attributable to pharmacy.
- Net profit and loss.
- Accounts receivable and payable.
- Supplier invoices, purchases and outstanding balances.
- Cash and bank transactions.
- Customer refunds, discounts and credit notes.
- Inventory valuation and stock adjustments.
- Tax liabilities and applicable tax reporting.
- Financial journals and transaction audit trails.
- Pharmacy-specific income statement, balance sheet and cash flow reporting, where supported by the accounting model.
- Financial period closing, reconciliations and reporting.

**Critical architectural requirement:**

Pharmacy accounting must be operationally and logically separated from laboratory accounting, but the application must still support a properly designed consolidated organization-wide general ledger.

For example:

- Pharmacy transactions must be attributed to the pharmacy business unit.
- Laboratory transactions must be attributed to the laboratory business unit.
- Imaging transactions must be attributed to the imaging business unit.
- Shared expenses must be allocated using explicit, configurable allocation rules.
- Consolidated financial statements must eliminate internal transfers and prevent double counting.

Do not create completely disconnected accounting databases or duplicate financial postings unless the existing architecture explicitly requires that approach.

Use business-unit dimensions, cost centers, source-module identifiers and appropriate ledger structures to preserve financial integrity.

### 1.2 Pharmacy Accounting UI

Create or improve a dedicated navigation section:

**Pharmacy → Accounting**

It should contain financial overview, revenue, expenses, payables, receivables, ledgers, financial statements, reconciliations and audit history, subject to existing features and permissions.

Do not present accounting statements merely as charts within Pharmacy Analytics.

Charts may summarize financial information, but detailed financial records and reports belong in the dedicated accounting section.

Ensure every financial figure is traceable to underlying transactions.

### 2.2 Google Analytics 4 as the Design Benchmark

**Redesign the administrative dashboard to provide an experience comparable to Google Analytics 4.**

Study GA4's presentation patterns, including:

- Clean, spacious and modern dashboard layouts.
- Interactive KPI summary cards.
- Smooth time-series line and area charts.
- Comparison against previous periods.
- Date-range controls.
- Interactive legends and tooltips.
- Drill-down into detailed reports.
- Clear visual hierarchy.
- Contextual trend indicators.
- Responsive chart containers.
- Consistent typography, spacing and color semantics.
- Customizable dashboard sections.

Do not simply copy the Google Analytics interface. Adapt its strongest analytical and interaction principles to a professional healthcare enterprise platform, preserving PLS branding.

### 2.3 Dashboard Layout Proposal

The administrative dashboard should have the following structure.

**Section A — Executive KPI Overview**

Display relevant KPI cards, such as:

- Total revenue.
- Total collections.
- Outstanding receivables.
- Total registered patients.
- Total laboratory requests.
- Completed laboratory tests.
- Imaging procedures performed.
- Pharmacy sales.
- Operating expenses.
- Gross profit or contribution margin, where applicable.

Each KPI should show its current value, comparison-period change, trend indicator and an appropriate link to its detailed report.

Only display KPIs for subscribed modules and authorized users.

**Section B — Revenue and Operational Trends**

Provide interactive time-series charts for revenue, collections, patient visits, test volumes and other relevant metrics.

Support daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly aggregation.

**Section C — Departmental Performance**

Show performance by authorized business unit, such as laboratory, pharmacy and imaging.

Use comparative charts where meaningful, while avoiding inappropriate mixing of unrelated measures.

**Section D — Operational Efficiency**

Include turnaround times, pending workloads, completed services, workflow bottlenecks and staff productivity, where supported by reliable data.

**Section E — Alerts and Insights**

Display actionable insights such as overdue receivables, unusually high cancellations, inventory expiry risks, stockouts and abnormal operational trends.

Insights must be based on verified data and explain the reason for the alert.

# PART 3: REDESIGN PHARMACY ANALYTICS

The pharmacy analytics dashboard must focus exclusively on pharmacy operations and performance.

Do not allow unrelated laboratory, imaging or general inventory information to appear in pharmacy analytics.

### 3.1 Pharmacy KPI Cards

Display:

- Total pharmacy sales.
- Number of pharmacy transactions.
- Total medicines dispensed.
- Average transaction value.
- Gross profit.
- Gross profit margin.
- Pharmacy refunds.
- Outstanding pharmacy receivables.
- Stock valuation.
- Expiring medicines.
- Low-stock medicines.
- Out-of-stock medicines.

Ensure the distinction between revenue, collections, profit and inventory value is clear.

### 3.2 Pharmacy Charts

Implement professional interactive charts covering:

| Chart                        | Purpose                                            |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Sales trend                  | Daily, weekly and monthly pharmacy sales           |
| Revenue vs COGS              | Pharmacy gross profitability                       |
| Top-selling medicines        | Highest-selling products by quantity or revenue    |
| Product category performance | Revenue and units by medicine category             |
| Stock movement               | Purchases, sales, returns and adjustments          |
| Inventory expiry             | Products approaching expiry                        |
| Payment methods              | Pharmacy collections by payment channel            |
| Profit margin trend          | Changes in pharmacy profitability                  |
| Supplier performance         | Purchase volumes and supplier delivery performance |
| Sales by branch              | Authorized branch-level comparisons                |

Use line, area, bar, stacked bar and donut charts according to the data being represented.

Avoid adding charts simply for decoration. Every chart must answer a meaningful business question.

### 3.3 Interactive Analytics

Support:

- Custom date ranges.
- Previous-period comparisons.
- Branch filtering.
- Medicine category filtering.
- Searchable tables.
- Drill-down from charts to transactions.
- Export to PDF, Excel or CSV, subject to permissions.
- Loading skeletons and informative empty states.
- Responsive layouts.
- Consistent currency and number formatting.

Clicking a sales trend point should allow authorized users to inspect the transactions contributing to that value.

# PART 4: REMOVE UNRELATED MODULE CONTENT

I have observed unrelated items appearing in the pharmacy module, including laboratory supplies, imaging supplies and an item labelled "money test" or similar.

Investigate exactly where these entries originate.

Determine whether they result from:

1. Incorrect navigation configuration.
2. Shared inventory categories without proper module scoping.
3. Missing backend filtering.
4. Incorrect joins or aggregation queries.
5. Hardcoded dashboard entries.
6. Shared components displaying inappropriate data.
7. Missing subscription checks.
8. Incorrect role permissions.

**Expected behavior:**

Pharmacy should display pharmacy-specific medicines, consumables, transactions and inventory.

Laboratory should display laboratory tests, reagents, laboratory consumables and related inventory.

Imaging should display imaging procedures, imaging consumables and related operational information.

Shared inventory may exist, but each module must access only its authorized and contextually relevant inventory records.

Do not delete existing records simply because they appear in the wrong interface. Correct the data classification, relationships, filters and access controls.

# PART 5: ENFORCE TRUE MODULAR ARCHITECTURE

PLS is a subscription-based modular platform.

A facility may subscribe to Pharmacy without Laboratory, Laboratory without Pharmacy, or any permitted combination of modules.

The system must behave correctly in every supported subscription configuration.

### 5.1 Module Entitlement Enforcement

Review the existing master-platform subscription and tenant entitlement mechanisms.

Enforce module access consistently across:

- Sidebar navigation.
- Dashboard widgets.
- Frontend routes.
- Backend API endpoints.
- Financial reports.
- Inventory queries.
- Analytics aggregation services.
- Exports.
- Background jobs.
- Inter-module integrations.

A hidden menu item is not a security boundary.

The backend must independently verify module entitlement, tenant identity and user permissions.

### 5.2 Authorization Model

Apply the existing role-based access control architecture.

Where appropriate, distinguish permissions such as:

- `pharmacy.view`
- `pharmacy.analytics.view`
- `pharmacy.accounting.view`
- `pharmacy.accounting.export`
- `pharmacy.transactions.manage`
- `laboratory.analytics.view`
- `imaging.analytics.view`
- `accounting.consolidated.view`

Treat these as suggested permission names; inspect and reuse the application's existing naming conventions.

A pharmacist should not automatically gain access to laboratory financial information.

Likewise, a laboratory user should not automatically gain access to pharmacy accounting information.

Organization-level financial administrators may have broader access when explicitly authorized.

### 5.3 Cross-Module Integration

Do not prohibit legitimate integrations.

For example, a laboratory patient may purchase medicines through pharmacy, or an organization may use a shared payment system.

Such workflows should use explicit service boundaries, transaction attribution and permission checks.

Cross-module integration must never result in uncontrolled data exposure.

# PART 8: PERFORMANCE AND SCALABILITY

The application must remain responsive as the number of tenants, transactions and users increases.

Inspect and improve:

- Slow aggregation queries.
- Missing database indexes.
- Excessive dashboard API calls.
- Duplicate requests.
- Unnecessary frontend rerenders.
- Inefficient joins.
- Unbounded report queries.

## FINAL INSTRUCTION

Do not approach this assignment as a cosmetic dashboard redesign alone.

This is an enterprise architecture, accounting integrity, modular authorization and analytics modernization exercise.

The goal is to make Prime Laboratory System function as a truly modular healthcare platform where each subscribed module operates independently, integrates safely with other authorized modules, and presents accurate, attractive and actionable analytics.

**The final product must combine the analytical clarity of Google Analytics, the financial discipline of enterprise accounting software, and the strict modularity required by a multi-tenant healthcare management platform.**

One architectural decision is particularly important for PLS: separate pharmacy and laboratory accounting by business unit, not necessarily by creating two unrelated accounting engines. This allows independent departmental reporting while preserving accurate consolidated financial statements for the facility.

I would also have the coding agent prioritize the subscription and authorization audit before the visual redesign. Otherwise, even a beautiful dashboard could continue displaying information from modules a facility has not subscribed to.

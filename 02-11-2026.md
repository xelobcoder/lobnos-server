The central issue is no longer whether PLS Pharmacy has enough features. It is whether users can understand those features and complete their work without extensive training.

A sophisticated system should not require a sophisticated user. The backend can handle double-entry accounting, inventory valuation, supplier liabilities, approval workflows and financial reconciliation. The frontend should simply help a pharmacist, cashier, storekeeper or accountant accomplish a task naturally.

I would approach this as a complete pharmacy user-experience and workflow redesign, not another feature-development exercise. The existing payroll, main accounting, laboratory, imaging, registration and central inventory modules should serve as references, not targets for unnecessary rebuilding.

Here is a stronger prompt for your coding agent.

# PLS PHARMACY — COMPLETE USER EXPERIENCE, WORKFLOW AND ACCOUNTING SIMPLIFICATION

## ROLE AND OBJECTIVE

Act as a principal product designer, senior UX researcher, enterprise software architect, pharmacy operations consultant, accounting systems specialist and full-stack engineer.

You are working on Prime Laboratory System (PLS).

Significant development work has already been completed, and the existing functionality deserves recognition. However, the Pharmacy module has a serious usability problem.

**Even the developer finds parts of the pharmacy workflow, procurement structure and pharmacy accounting difficult to understand.**

This indicates that the system may be technically sophisticated but is not adequately translating that sophistication into a clear, intuitive user experience.

Your responsibility is to investigate and correct this problem comprehensively.

The objective is not to reduce functionality. The objective is to reduce the complexity users experience.

### Fundamental design principle

**Make the frontend simple, predictable and understandable while preserving the full sophistication of the backend.**

A first-time user should be able to understand where to go, what to do next, what each action means and what happens after completing it.

Use principles associated with Figma-quality product design, progressive disclosure, recognition over recall, consistency, clear visual hierarchy and task-oriented navigation.

Do not interpret this as a request to copy Figma's interface.

# 2. PERFORM A COMPLETE PHARMACY USABILITY AUDIT

Before changing code, examine the entire Pharmacy module from the perspective of someone using it for the first time.

Do not evaluate only whether buttons work.

Evaluate whether the workflow makes sense.

For every page, determine:

- What is the purpose of this page?
- Who is expected to use it?
- What task does the user want to accomplish?
- Is that task obvious immediately?
- Are the labels understandable?
- Are there unnecessary technical or accounting terms?
- Are actions arranged logically?
- Does the user know what happens next?
- Is information duplicated across pages?
- Are there too many menu items?
- Are users forced to understand backend structures?
- Are there unnecessary forms or confirmation screens?
- Are there dead ends?
- Can users recover easily from mistakes?
- Does the page provide clear success and error feedback?

Identify pages that are technically functional but operationally confusing.

Document the specific problem, its root cause and a proposed improvement.

# 3. REDESIGN PHARMACY NAVIGATION AROUND USER TASKS

Do not organize the frontend merely according to backend modules, database tables or controller names.

Organize the Pharmacy interface around the activities people actually perform.

Proposed top-level navigation:

### Pharmacy

**Overview**

A simple summary of today's pharmacy activities, sales, pending tasks and stock alerts.

**Sales & Dispensing**

POS, counter invoices, sales history, customer returns and credit sales where permitted.

**Stock & Medicines**

Medicine catalog, stock balances, batches, expiry dates, stock adjustments and stock movement.

**Purchasing**

Purchase requests, purchase orders, goods received, supplier invoices and purchasing history.

**Suppliers**

Supplier directory, supplier profiles and relevant supplier transaction history.

**Pharmacy Accounting**

A dedicated financial management workspace with simple navigation and complete accounting functionality.

**Reports**

Pharmacy sales, stock, purchasing, operational and financial reports, grouped logically.

**Help & Learning**

Interactive walkthroughs, workflow explanations and a searchable user guide.

Avoid excessive nesting.

Avoid displaying every accounting function in the main Pharmacy sidebar.

Use a small number of recognizable categories with clear page-level navigation.

Apply permission and subscription controls to every relevant page and action.

### Proposed user-facing purchasing workflow

\<escape>Step 2 — Review and Approve\</escape>

\<escape>Step 3 — Order from Supplier\</escape>

\<escape>Step 6 — Pay Supplier\</escape>

Support partial deliveries, rejected deliveries, purchase returns and supplier credit notes without confusing the main workflow.

Do not change accounting recognition rules merely to simplify the interface.

# 5. COMPLETELY RECONSIDER PHARMACY ACCOUNTING UX

This is the highest-priority usability problem.

The current Pharmacy Accounting interface is too difficult to navigate and understand.

Do not assume that because a feature exists, it is easy to use.

Do not assume that adding charts, tables or more menu items will solve the problem.

### Design around questions users naturally ask

A pharmacy manager may want to know:

- How much did we sell today?
- How much money have we collected?
- Who owes us money?
- Which suppliers do we owe?
- What have we spent?
- Are we making a profit?
- How much is our stock worth?
- Where did a particular payment go?

Make these questions easy to answer.

### Proposed Pharmacy Accounting homepage

Present a clear financial summary followed by recognizable actions:

**Money In**

Sales revenue, collections, customer payments and receivables.

**Money Out**

Supplier payments, operating expenses and other authorized payments.

**People Who Owe Us**

Customer invoices, outstanding balances, overdue accounts and payment recording.

**Suppliers We Owe**

Supplier bills, due payments, aging and supplier settlement.

**Expenses**

Record, approve, pay and review pharmacy operating expenses.

**Profit & Performance**

Revenue, COGS, gross profit, operating expenses and net profit.

**Accounting Records**

Chart of accounts, journals, general ledger and trial balance.

**Financial Reports**

Income statement, balance sheet, cash flow reports where supported and reconciliation reports.

### Critical requirement

These simple labels are presentation choices.

They must not replace the underlying accounting structures.

For example, selecting "People Who Owe Us" must open a fully functional Accounts Receivable workspace.

Selecting "Suppliers We Owe" must open Accounts Payable.

Selecting "Accounting Records" must provide access to real journal entries and the general ledger.

Provide clear formal accounting labels within the page for trained finance professionals.

---

# 6. APPLY PROGRESSIVE DISCLOSURE

Do not expose every technical option to every user.

Use a layered experience.

### Basic operational experience

Designed for cashiers, pharmacists and storekeepers.

Show only the tasks relevant to their roles.

### Management experience

Designed for pharmacy managers.

Provide sales, expenses, supplier balances, stock performance, approvals and financial summaries.

### Professional accounting experience

Designed for accountants and authorized finance personnel.

Provide the full chart of accounts, journals, general ledger, trial balance, reconciliations, financial statements and period controls.

These are interface and permission-aware views, not separate accounting engines.

All views must use the same authoritative financial data.

Do not hide essential information needed for safe or compliant task completion.

# 7. SIMPLIFY FORMS AND TRANSACTION ENTRY

Review every pharmacy form.

The system should determine appropriate financial postings from validated configuration and transaction context.

Where additional information is necessary, show it at the relevant step.

Use searchable medicine selectors, supplier selectors, inline validation and meaningful error messages.

Preserve advanced settings for authorized users who genuinely need them.

- Time to complete a task.

# FINAL ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

The redesign is successful only when:

- A first-time pharmacy user can identify where to perform common tasks.
- The purchasing workflow follows a clear, logical sequence.
- Users understand the current status and next action for each purchase.
- Pharmacy Accounting is easy to navigate without sacrificing accounting functionality.
- Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable are clearly distinguishable.
- Users can locate expenses, supplier payments and financial reports quickly.
- Technical accounting complexity is appropriately hidden from operational users.
- Professional accountants retain access to complete financial controls.
- Every important pharmacy workflow has an accessible instructional guide.
- Existing PLS modules remain operational.
- Financial and inventory reconciliation tests pass.
- Module subscriptions and role permissions remain enforced.
- The redesigned UI has been evaluated through realistic user tasks.

## FINAL DIRECTIVE

**Do not respond to this assignment by adding more features, charts, menus or complex pages.**

The primary problem is usability.

Study the existing Pharmacy module carefully, understand why users struggle and redesign the experience around the real work performed in a pharmacy.

Do not confuse technical completeness with product usability.

A user should not need to understand the internal architecture of PLS to sell medicine, request stock, pay a supplier, record an expense or understand pharmacy profitability.

Keep the backend sophisticated.

Keep the financial controls rigorous.

Keep the frontend simple.

**The ultimate objective is to make PLS Pharmacy feel easy to use from the very first interaction, even though it is powered by a comprehensive enterprise system.**

Begin by producing the critical usability audit, proposed navigation hierarchy and redesigned user journeys before making substantial code changes.

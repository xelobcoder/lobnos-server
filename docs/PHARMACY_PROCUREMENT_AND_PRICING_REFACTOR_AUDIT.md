# PLS Pharmacy procurement and pricing refactor audit

## 1. Scope, evidence and implementation status

This audit compares Pharmacy with **Main Inventory (laboratory/general inventory)**. It covers internal requests, supplier orders, deliveries, costs, selling prices, supplier invoices, valuation and payment integration in both backend and frontend repositories.

This is the pre-implementation report requested for the procurement refactor. The proposed contracts, tables and policies below are **not implemented or deployed**. Earlier purchase-entry UI improvements and the audited administrator self-review exception already exist and must be preserved.

Evidence comes from source inspection and the focused tests identified in section 10. Tests use isolated fixtures, mocks and in-memory storage; they do not prove live MySQL migration correctness or authenticated end-to-end operation. No live tenant data was changed, no migration was executed, and no existing financial records were reconciled.

Links starting `../src/` refer to this backend. Links starting `../../lobnos/` refer to the sibling frontend repository.

## 2. Executive findings

1. **Pharmacy Purchase Request is currently a hybrid internal requisition and supplier quotation.** It is stored as `purchase_requisition`, but supplier and quoted unit costs are mandatory. Approval immediately creates an ordered supplier purchase order. This collapses two decisions that should be separate.
2. **Partial and multiple Pharmacy deliveries already work.** The problem is not the absence of partial receiving: order-linked receipts reject cumulative excess quantities and any deviation from the agreed unit cost.
3. **Physical delivery and usable stock are currently one event.** There is no procurement receipt record with separately controlled delivered, accepted, held and returned quantities.
4. **Pharmacy receiving already has stronger idempotency than Main Inventory receiving.** Replacing it with Main Inventory's purchase-order implementation would remove important protections.
5. **Main Inventory pricing adjustments are not purchase-cost markups.** They increase the previous selling price. They cannot be reused unchanged for fixed markup, percentage markup or target gross margin calculated from actual purchase cost.
6. **Both modules currently recognize supplier liability at goods receipt.** Pharmacy invoice matching is evidence-only; registering a receipt as a payable bill does not create another liability.
7. **Main Inventory is useful prior art, not a fully reliable procurement engine to transplant.** Its PO editing, changing-price receipt totals, cancellation and payment allocations have source-confirmed limitations. They are outside the scope of Pharmacy fixes.
8. The refactor should reuse existing arithmetic, transactions, payment controls and UI patterns while keeping Pharmacy tables, batches, permissions and accounting attribution separate.

## 4. Detailed Pharmacy findings

### P1. Request, quotation and order are conflated

[PharmacyProcurement.request:6](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyProcurement.ts#L6) requires an active branch supplier and numeric quoted unit cost for every line. It stores the request and an initial quotation together. [decide:18](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyProcurement.ts#L18) validates a selected quotation and creates the supplier order on approval.

The public [POST /orders adapter:112](../src/controllers/pharmacy/routes.ts#L112) also dispatches to `procurement.request`, despite its order-shaped route name. [PharmacyManagement.createOrder:580](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyManagement.ts#L580) separately contains order creation used by tests. These are overlapping implementations, not two user-facing stages.

**Required change:** a request contains medicines, requested quantities, units, purpose and optional estimates. Missing estimate means unknown, not zero. Approval records approved quantities and amendments without placing an order. A subsequent authorized action selects supplier, quantities and commercial terms and issues the order. Existing approved requests with linked orders must not create another order.

Keep the existing independent review rule and explicit audited administrator self-review exception. Do not silently grant ordinary managers self-approval.

### P2. Order receiving blocks real deliveries and price differences

[PharmacyManagement.receive:350](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyManagement.ts#L350) rejects an order receipt when actual unit cost differs from the order or cumulative received quantity exceeds the order. It also rejects purchase-pack mode for order-linked receiving.

The frontend [order receipt form:54](../../lobnos/app/pharmacy/components/PharmacyInventoryWorkspace.tsx#L54) caps quantity at remaining units and submits the agreed cost rather than an editable actual cost.

Partial receipts and distinct retries already work. Remove neither cumulative controls nor branch/supplier/item validation. Introduce a policy-based acceptance decision around the actual delivery.

### P3. Current quarantine is not an unaccepted-delivery holding area

[PharmacyStockWorkflows.quarantine:60](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyStockWorkflows.ts#L60) removes existing inventory and posts a transfer to quarantine inventory. Release moves it back and credits quarantine inventory, or cost of sales for customer returns.

A supplier delivery not yet accepted is not already owned inventory with that accounting history. Reusing the quarantine release routine unchanged would invent valuation or incorrect credits. Reuse its inspection, batch, recall and expiry safeguards, but model procurement acceptance holds separately.

### P4. Valuation and idempotency must survive

The [document wrapper:308](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyManagement.ts#L308) fingerprints the operation and commits its result transactionally. Matching retries recover the original result; payload mismatches fail.

Receiving locks stock, respects stock-count freezes, validates expiry and supplier, snapshots conversion and batch data, calculates weighted-average base cost and writes the movement and journal. See [receipt validation:350](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyManagement.ts#L350), [valuation:433](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyManagement.ts#L433), and [movement/journal/order updates:460](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyManagement.ts#L460).

Reuse [PharmacyRules:4](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyRules.ts#L4) for Decimal money, quantity and pack conversion. Existing precision is three decimals for quantities, six for base costs and two for money. Do not copy Main Inventory's lower cost precision or mix purchase/base-unit quantities.

### P5. Pricing surfaces are inconsistent

[PharmacyStockUpModal:20](../../lobnos/app/pharmacy/components/PharmacyStockUpModal.tsx#L20) supports keeping existing prices or submitting manual retail/wholesale prices. [PharmacyManagement:444](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyManagement.ts#L444) directly applies those prices when `change_pricing` is set. The separate order receipt form has no equivalent pricing step.

Existing item edits retain old/new values in [profile audit:156](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyManagement.ts#L156), but the inspected receipt price-update block does not create the same price-history record. Receipt-driven price updates also need an explicit server-side pricing permission check, not only permission to receive stock.

Historical sale prices and costs are snapshots in [PharmacySaleRepository.insertSaleLines:115](../src/models/pharmacy/repositories/PharmacySaleRepository.ts#L115). Never update them when catalogue prices change.

### P6. Invoice matching currently creates neither a new bill nor a new liability

[PharmacyEnterpriseManagement.purchaseInvoice:49](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyEnterpriseManagement.ts#L49) accepts one supplier's distinct receipt IDs, requires exact equality to receipt totals and stores a matched workflow. A receipt cannot be matched again. There are no invoice-line tax/discount/cost-variance calculations in this method.

Receiving already posts Dr inventory / Cr supplier payable at [PharmacyManagement:481](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyManagement.ts#L481). [registerBill:19](../src/models/pharmacy/accounting/PharmacyAccountingSubledgers.ts#L19) verifies this liability and registers a receipt-backed bill without reposting it. [payables:34](../src/models/pharmacy/accounting/PharmacyAccountingRegisters.ts#L34) derives supplier balances from journals and exposes receipt/bill/allocation relationships.

The [accounting bridge:64](../src/models/pharmacy/PharmacyAccountingBridge.ts#L64) synchronizes journals and AP entries transactionally when enabled. A second invoice credit to AP would duplicate both the liability and potentially the shared AP entry.

**Required change:** version invoice recognition and source provenance explicitly. Historical receipt-backed liabilities, payments and allocations remain authoritative. New invoice-backed bills must use the existing allocation and settlement controls, not a second payment system.

## 6. Proposed policy defaults and state model

Policy questions were presented, but the user was unavailable. The following are conservative **design recommendations**, not confirmed facility policy and not activated configuration.

### Requests and orders

`Requested -> Approved / Rejected -> Ordered -> Partially received -> Delivery complete`

- Approval may amend quantities with a reason, preserving requested and approved values.
- A request can feed multiple supplier orders up to the approved quantities. Lock conversion and record allocated order quantities; retries must not allocate twice.
- Preserve existing quotation evidence. An old approved request already linked to an order is already converted.
- Supplier is mandatory at order issue, not requisition. Estimated and agreed costs are distinct. If a cost is still unknown, mark it unknown explicitly; require confirmed valuation before accepting purchased stock.
- Short-close requires manager authority, a reason and an auditable remaining-quantity disposition.

### Delivery and acceptance

`Physical delivery recorded -> Inspection/cost review -> Accepted / Partly accepted / Held -> Released / Returned`

- Persist actual delivered quantity even when acceptance policy prohibits excess.
- Proposed default: zero automatic over-receipt tolerance; hold excess outside usable inventory until manager/admin review.
- Configurable modes: prohibit excess acceptance; accept within tolerance and hold beyond it; require approval for every excess unit.
- Tolerance is cumulative against the supplier order quantity, never reset per delivery. For order quantity 100 and tolerance 10%, automatic cumulative acceptance cannot exceed 110.
- Compare against the actual order, not the earlier requisition. Record the applied policy/version on the delivery.
- For each delivery line, `delivered = accepted + held + returned`; rejected awaiting physical return remains held with an explicit disposition. All values use base units with snapshotted purchase-unit conversion.
- Held goods are visible in physical custody but excluded from dispensable inventory, accepted-stock valuation and AP. Expired/recalled/damaged goods cannot become dispensable merely through excess approval.
- Physical evidence creation and acceptance are separate idempotent operations. Acceptance atomically changes stock, valuation, movements, order accepted counters and journals. If acceptance fails, retain the recorded delivery and show an explicit pending/error state, not "stock received".
- Receiving an extra delivery after the ordered quantity is met must remain discoverable; do not hide the action just because ordered quantities are fulfilled.

### Cost and price policy

- Preserve original agreed cost. Actual delivered/accepted cost, invoice cost and variance reason are separate fields.
- Allocate explicit discounts and landed charges deterministically across accepted lines. Recoverable tax is not inventory cost; nonrecoverable tax follows configured tax treatment. Never invent tax rates or recoverability.
- Unknown actual cost creates a valuation hold, not an invented zero-cost receipt. Genuine free stock needs explicit evidence.
- Default selling-price choice is **Keep existing**. Manager/admin authority is required for an actual price change.
- Proposed default: immediate confirmed changes, two-decimal half-up rounding, complete old/new price history. Configurable rounding increments and future-dated approved prices are a separate extension, not silently enabled.
- No medicine price-cap or jurisdiction-specific compliance claim is made without facility/regulatory requirements.

For base-unit landed cost `C`:

| Method | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Keep existing | No price mutation | Existing price stays unchanged |
| Manual | Explicit proposed price | 30.00 |
| Fixed markup | `C + amount` | 20 + 5 = 25.00 |
| Percentage markup | `C * (1 + percent / 100)` | 20 * 1.25 = 25.00 |
| Target gross margin | `C / (1 - percent / 100)` | 20 / 0.80 = 25.00 |

Reject nonfinite input, negative markup and margin outside `[0,100)`. Validate range and rounding on the server. Preview cost basis, current price, proposed price and realized margin after rounding. For multiple accepted batches of one medicine, use the quantity-weighted landed cost of those accepted delivery lines for the proposed catalogue-price calculation; do not let the last batch silently win. Retail and wholesale remain distinct. A receipt with no accepted units must not change selling prices.

## 7. Financial recognition and historical compatibility

### Recommended new-version model

For new procurement after explicit cutover:

1. Physical delivery/hold: evidence only; no usable-stock asset or supplier AP yet.
2. Accepted goods with confirmed cost: Dr inventory / Cr goods received not invoiced (GRNI).
3. Matched supplier invoice: clear matched GRNI, recognize configured recoverable tax/authorized adjustments and credit supplier AP.
4. Supplier payment: existing approved full/partial bill allocation and funding-account posting.

Receiving and invoice entry may be presented together, but retain separate linked documents and posting sources. Accepted stock can become usable before the invoice arrives; the frontend must explain the difference without exposing journal terminology.

Use the existing journal and bridge, not a second ledger. A classified liability account such as `liability:grni` requires active GL mappings when bridge posting is enabled, report/cash-flow classification and reconciliation tests. Never enable new posting against incomplete mappings.

Invoice-price discrepancies must be explicit. Initially block posting unresolved discrepancies until authorized cost-adjustment or supplier-credit evidence exists. Do not silently expense all differences, capitalize sold quantities, rewrite historical sale costs, or change closed-period journals. A later automatic inventory/COGS allocation requires tested receipt/lot consumption attribution and accountant-approved rules.

### Compatibility and cutover

- Keep all old receipt journals, receipt-backed bills, due dates, payments and allocations unchanged.
- Persist a recognition version/source on each new document; historical documents remain receipt-recognized.
- Matching an invoice against a legacy AP-recognized receipt must not post AP again. Surface existing bill and settlement links.
- Do not mix legacy-recognized and new GRNI-recognized lines in one invoice posting until a tested split-clearing contract exists.
- Require dry-run reconciliation of stock, GRNI, supplier AP, local/shared journal links and bill allocations before enablement.
- No automatic historical backfill or live cutover is authorized by this audit.
- Disabling new entry must not make new-version bills or held deliveries inaccessible. After postings exist, rollback means controlled forward corrections, not deletion or dropping tables.

## 8. Proposed schema and API changes

Names below describe a proposed contract, not existing endpoints.

### Additive data changes

- Version requisition details; retain original requests/quotes and add approved quantities, approval amendments and order-allocation links.
- Add explicit requisition linkage and immutable agreed commercial snapshots to orders. Keep order commitment totals separate from actual receipt and invoice totals.
- Add delivery headers and batch-level lines with supplier/order linkage, delivery reference, delivered/accepted/held/returned quantities, conversion, condition, batch, expiry, actual cost, valuation status, policy snapshot and version.
- Add unique acceptance/disposition event sources for receipt retries, held releases and supplier returns.
- Add supplier invoice headers/lines and receipt-line allocations: invoice reference/date/due date, discounts/taxes/charges, variance evidence, recognition version, journal source and payable bill linkage.
- Add price-change history/proposals: old/new retail and wholesale prices, method, parameter, cost basis, rounding, actor, approval, effective timestamp and receipt linkage.
- Add branch procurement settings with versioned changes. Follow the repository's guarded schema-upgrade mechanism; do not introduce an unrelated migration framework.
- Database uniqueness must protect conversion, receipt/acceptance sources, normalized supplier invoice references and journal/bill sources, in addition to request fingerprints.
- Preserve existing quantity/cost precision and supported identifier sizes. Add indexes for branch, order line, receipt line and supplier lookups.

### API surface

| Proposed operation | Contract |
|---|---|
| Request medicines | Lines and requested quantities; optional estimates/supplier suggestion; no mandatory final cost |
| Review request | Expected version, approve/modify/reject, approved quantities, reason, existing self-review rules |
| Convert to supplier order | Approved request allocation, supplier, agreed quantities/terms, expected version and request key |
| Record delivery | Actual batch lines and physical quantities; expected order version, policy snapshot and request key |
| Accept/release/return delivery | Explicit quantities, inspection/cost evidence and authorized discrepancy decision; separate idempotent source |
| Preview/confirm price | Server-calculated method, cost basis, old/new prices and required pricing authority |
| Record/post invoice | Supplier invoice lines, receipt allocations, configured tax/cost evidence and explicit recognition source |
| Settle invoice | Existing Pharmacy AP bill/settlement routes, not a new supplier-payment engine |

Keep legacy routes as explicit compatibility adapters with visible semantics. New UI must use the unambiguous contracts. Do not infer request version from missing supplier/cost fields, or silently change an old client's approval into a different transaction.

## 9. Simple user-facing flow

Use the existing Pharmacy visual system, wide modal and persistent medicine-picker behavior.

1. **Request medicines:** medicine -> quantity -> Add; optional estimate under secondary details. Preserve focus and the editable basket.
2. **Review request:** original/requested versus approved quantities; approve, amend or reject with reason.
3. **Order from supplier:** choose approved request and supplier; review commercial terms; issue order.
4. **Receive delivery:** select order or explicit no-order delivery; enter actual batch quantities, expiry and actual cost. Show Ordered, Delivered, Accepted and Awaiting review as labeled values.
5. **Review prices:** default Keep existing; opt-in cost-based calculation and impact preview.
6. **Record supplier invoice:** link delivery lines, show discrepancies, post or explain what must be resolved.
7. **Confirm result:** accepted stock, pending holds, linked invoice and next action.
8. **Pay supplier:** open existing Pharmacy Payables with the exact bill, outstanding amount and full/partial settlement.

Show one primary next action and compact status summaries. Put advanced tax/cost breakdowns behind progressive disclosure. Do not restore large instructional blocks removed from Inventory; retain contextual help and update the practical manual.

## 11. Implementation sequence

1. Approve contracts and policy defaults, then add versioned schema and compatibility fixtures.
2. Separate requisition approval from supplier order conversion, preserving existing linked orders.
3. Introduce physical delivery/acceptance and configurable excess policy, keeping acceptance transactional.
4. Consolidate receiving UI and add confirmed costs, batch lines and server-authoritative pricing/history.
5. Add financial invoice matching, GRNI cutover guards and existing-AP bill integration.
6. Update connected navigation/manuals and complete reconciliation, permissions, retry, migration and browser validation.

Do not enable the new entry flow halfway through this sequence. Keep old records readable and old behavior explicitly versioned until the new delivery, invoice and settlement contracts pass reconciliation.
